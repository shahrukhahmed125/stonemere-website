Sound familiar?

When everyday life becomes difficult to manage

It rarely happens all at once. A hospital stay, a fall, a diagnosis or a bereavement, and suddenly there are a dozen things to organise and nobody quite sure who's doing what.

  • Mum's coming home from hospital on Friday, and I live 200 miles away.
  • The post is piling up and the bills have become confusing.
  • Dad needs to downsize, but the house holds fifty years of belongings.
  • We keep repeating the same story to the GP, the council and the care agency.
  • A tradesperson needs letting in, and there's no one local to do it.
  • The house needs sorting after a bereavement, and the family is scattered.

These aren't care problems. They're life-management problems, and that's exactly what we do.

01

Our approach

What is life management?

Life management brings together three things that are usually handled separately, or not at all. Think of us as the keystone: the stone at the top of a Bath arch that holds everything else in place.

  1. We speak up with you

    We help you communicate your wishes and deal with organisations, so you're listened to and understood.

  2. We bring everyone together

    Family, healthcare, social care, housing, charities, solicitors, tradespeople and suppliers, all coordinated.

  3. We follow it through

    We arrange services, source what's needed and chase every matter until it's resolved, not just passed on.

Case studies

Life management in practice

Every situation is different, but the approach is the same: listen, plan, coordinate and follow through. Drag or use the arrows to explore.

  • Hospital dischargeExample situation

    Home from hospital, with family 300 miles away

    The situation
    Margaret, 84, was ready for discharge after a fall. Her son lives in Newcastle.
    What we coordinated
    Home prepared, key safe and grab rails arranged, transport booked, a first shop delivered and a GP follow-up booked.
    The outcome
    Margaret came home on the planned day, and her son had a daily update for the first week.
  • DownsizingExample situation

    Fifty years of belongings, one calm move

    The situation
    A couple leaving a four-bedroom family home for a two-bedroom flat.
    What we coordinated
    Room-by-room sorting at their pace, storage for family items, removals, and the new flat set up before moving day.
    The outcome
    They slept in their new home on the first night with everything they needed unpacked.
  • After a bereavementExample situation

    A house to clear, with family overseas

    The situation
    An executor in Canada needed a Bath property cleared and prepared for sale.
    What we coordinated
    Belongings catalogued and photographed, keepsakes shipped, items donated and recycled, the property cleaned and handed to the agent.
    The outcome
    The family made every decision from abroad, and nothing of sentimental value was lost.
  • CrisisExample situation

    A burst pipe, a hospital stay, and a dog to look after

    The situation
    While Ruth, 81, was in hospital, a pipe burst at her home in Bear Flat. Her son was abroad.
    What we coordinated
    An emergency plumber, the call to her insurer, care for her dog, repairs and daily updates to her son.
    The outcome
    By the time Ruth was ready for discharge, her home was dry, safe and ready.
  • Family supportExample situation

    Three siblings, three countries, one plan

    The situation
    Siblings in London, Dubai and Toronto were supporting their father in Combe Down after a dementia diagnosis.
    What we coordinated
    A family call, home safety changes, memory-clinic appointments, a care-provider introduction and a shared monthly update.
    The outcome
    One plan, one source of information, and far fewer late-night phone calls.

No. We don't provide personal care, nursing or medical treatment. We provide practical life management: coordinating, organising and resolving the practical side of life. Many of our clients also receive care, and we work alongside their care provider.

Older people who want to stay independent, vulnerable or disabled adults who need practical support, and families who need a trusted person on the ground. We also accept referrals from health, care, housing, charity and legal professionals.

We're based in Bath and focus on clients within about five miles of the city and across Bath and North East Somerset. For existing clients and their families, we can coordinate matters further afield, including shipping belongings overseas.

Our services work on a simple membership: payments go into your own client account and are used for the time and services we provide. Transport, procurement, third-party costs and Crisis Management are charged separately. You'll find full details on our membership and pricing page.

Many people don't want to feel looked after, and we understand that completely. We work with the person, at their pace, and always respect their right to make their own decisions. Often the first conversation is simply about one practical problem, and trust grows from there.

Yes, practically. We make homes safer and easier to manage, coordinate appointments and services, and keep families informed, always working alongside the person's health and care professionals and respecting their wishes.

If someone is in immediate danger, call 999. For urgent medical advice, call NHS 111. For urgent practical problems, such as a sudden hospital admission or a home that isn't safe to return to, our Crisis Management service can help. It is charged separately.
